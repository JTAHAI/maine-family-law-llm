Title 19-A: Domestic Relations

Chapter 55: Rights and Responsibilities

Section marker: 19-A M.R.S. chapter 55 concerns parental rights and responsibilities.

§1653. Parental rights and responsibilities

Best interest of child; subsection 3

In making an award of parental rights and responsibilities with respect to a child, the court applies the standard of the best interest of the child. In decisions regarding the child's residence and parent-child contact, the court considers as primary the safety and well-being of the child.

The court considers these factors under 19-A M.R.S. § 1653(3):

  1. A. The age of the child.
  2. B. The relationship of the child with the child's parents and any other persons who may significantly affect the child's welfare.
  3. C. The preference of the child, if old enough to express a meaningful preference.
  4. D. The duration and adequacy of the child's current living arrangements and the desirability of maintaining continuity.
  5. E. The stability of any proposed living arrangements for the child.
  6. F. The motivation of the parties involved and their capacities to give the child love, affection and guidance.
  7. G. The child's adjustment to the child's present home, school and community.
  8. H. The capacity of each parent to allow and encourage frequent and continuing contact between the child and the other parent, including physical access.
  9. I. The capacity of each parent to cooperate or to learn to cooperate in child care.
  10. J. Methods for assisting parental cooperation and resolving disputes and each parent's willingness to use those methods.
  11. K. The effect on the child if one parent has sole authority over the child's upbringing.
  12. L. The existence of domestic abuse between the parents, in the past or currently, and how that abuse affects the child emotionally, the safety of the child, and the other best-interest factors.
  13. M. The existence of any history of child abuse by a parent.
  14. N. All other factors having a reasonable bearing on the physical and psychological well-being of the child.
  15. O. A parent's prior willful misuse of the protection from abuse process to gain tactical advantage, if established by clear and convincing evidence and supported by required findings.
  16. P. If the child is under one year of age, whether the child is being breast-fed.
  17. Q. The existence of a parent's conviction for a sex offense or sexually violent offense.
  18. R. If a person resides with a parent, whether that person has certain criminal, juvenile, or child-protection adjudication history involving sexual offenses.
  19. S. Whether allocation of some or all parental rights and responsibilities would best support the child's safety and well-being.

Related reminders

Subsection 4 says the court may not apply a preference for one parent over the other because of the parent's gender or the child's age or gender.

Subsection 5-A says that although a court considers that a protective order was issued, it determines parental rights and contact de novo and may not use the protective-order award as precedent.
