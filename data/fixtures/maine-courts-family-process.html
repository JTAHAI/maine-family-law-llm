Court Process in a Family Matters Case

Starting a family matter

Family matters may require court forms, service, scheduling, mediation, and court review.

Paper forms are available from district court clerks, and some forms can be downloaded from the Judicial Branch forms page.

Changing or enforcing an order

Parties may need to use the proper motion process to change or enforce an existing family order.

Service, deadlines, and hearing logistics

Family matter procedure can involve served papers, summons, response deadline risk, proof of service, and hearing or continuance questions. If service is disputed, incomplete, out-of-state service is needed, or a party was unable to serve, users should verify the current official rules and court forms before relying on a deadline or method.

Family matters may also involve electronic filing, eCourts or Odyssey record access, private record concerns, sealed record limits, filing fee or fee waiver forms, and clerk logistics. Court clerks can help with forms and logistics but cannot choose legal claims or strategy.

Parentage questions should be separated from divorce when parents were unmarried. Parentage, parental rights, child support, and existing family orders may require different forms and review.

A request to continue a hearing because of a service defect, disputed notice, or incomplete service should be handled as a court-process and deadline-risk issue for qualified review.
