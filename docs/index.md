    <div class="section-intro">
      <div class="eyebrow">Why this exists</div>
      <h2>Families should not lose the story because the record is scattered.</h2>
      <p class="lead">Court filings, orders, email threads, school records, provider notes, screenshots, receipts, and timelines often live in different places. The workbench helps a user bring those records into one structured, reviewable workspace without treating model memory as legal authority.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid-3">
      <article class="card">
        <div class="card-number">01</div>
        <h3>Organize the record</h3>
        <p>Build a read-only inventory of selected source files, create hashes, track provenance, and preserve a clear separation between originals and generated work products.</p>
      </article>
      <article class="card">
        <div class="card-number">02</div>
        <h3>Connect facts to evidence</h3>
        <p>Build timelines, issue trees, missing-record checklists, fact-to-evidence maps, and source-linked review packets.</p>
      </article>
      <article class="card">
        <div class="card-number">03</div>
        <h3>Check the legal support</h3>
        <p>Retrieve Maine statutes, rules, forms, and cases; inspect citations and quote spans; and block unsupported filing-ready claims.</p>
      </article>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section--wash">
  <div class="shell">
    <div class="section-intro">
      <div class="eyebrow">A workbench, not a generic chatbot</div>
      <h2>Source correctness matters more than polished prose.</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="grid-2">
      <div class="callout callout--teal">
        <h3>What the project is designed to do</h3>
        <ul class="check-list">
          <li>Answer Maine family-law questions with visible source cards.</li>
          <li>Review documents for issue labels, posture, red flags, and missing information.</li>
          <li>Build timelines, authority matrices, and evidence maps.</li>
          <li>Check citations, quote spans, source freshness, and filing-readiness blockers.</li>
          <li>Create working motions, affidavits, letters, objections, findings, and checklists.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
      <div class="callout">
        <h3>What it does not promise</h3>
        <ul class="check-list">
          <li>It does not replace a lawyer or form an attorney-client relationship.</li>
          <li>It does not guarantee that a source, conclusion, draft, or export is correct.</li>
          <li>It does not treat model memory as legal authority.</li>
          <li>It does not silently convert a failed review gate into a filing-ready result.</li>
          <li>It does not put private matter files, runtime databases, or model weights in the public repository.</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
